# Payung

[![Tests](https://github.com/PugarHuda/payung/actions/workflows/test.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/PugarHuda/payung/actions/workflows/test.yml) [![Keeper](https://github.com/PugarHuda/payung/actions/workflows/keeper.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/PugarHuda/payung/actions/workflows/keeper.yml)

**Liquidation cover for Mezo MUSD borrowers.** *Payung* is Indonesian for umbrella.

Mezo lets you live off your Bitcoin without selling it: lock BTC, borrow MUSD. The one thing that can take your BTC away is a price drop to the liquidation line. Today a borrower's only defences are watching the price or borrowing less. Payung lets them **buy protection** instead.

- **Borrowers** pay a premium in MUSD for one to four weeks of cover. If the Mezo BTC price touches their strike before the cover's expiry (a Thursday 00:00 UTC, the Mezo Earn epoch boundary), Payung pays a fixed MUSD amount **straight into their trove** through Mezo's own `repayMUSDWithSignature`, lifting the collateral ratio before liquidation.
- **Underwriters** deposit MUSD into an ERC-4626 vault (`pyMUSD`) and earn the premiums. It is a new MUSD yield source with its own risk profile, uncorrelated with Stability Pool liquidations or LP fees, and with a hard cap on how much it can lose in one week.

Built for the [Mezo Buildathon](https://app.akindo.io/wave-hacks/OVOO0gdrVU8379D10), Track 1 (DeFi).

## How it works

```
underwriters ──MUSD──▶ Payung vault (ERC-4626) ◀──premium── borrower
                          │  backs every policy, open cover ≤ 50% of the vault
   Mezo PriceFeed ──▶ poke()             permissionless: on-chain BTC volatility
                  ──▶ trigger(strike)    permissionless, once price ≤ strike; caller earns 0.1%
                          │
                      claim(policy)      permissionless (keeper)
                          ├─ repayMUSDWithSignature(cover, borrower, sig) ──▶ borrower's trove
                          └─ fallback: MUSD to the borrower's wallet
```

1. **Buy.** `buy(expiry, strike, cover, maxPremium, autoRepay, sigDeadline, signature)`. `expiry` is this week's Thursday or any of the next three, so cover runs one to four weeks. Strikes sit on a $1,000 grid, 5% to 30% below spot. With `autoRepay`, the borrower signs Mezo's `RepayMUSD(amount, borrower, nonce, deadline)` once in the app. `maxPremium` protects the buyer if BTC moves between quote and execution.
2. **Price.** A one-touch probability depends on distance / (volatility × √time). Payung rescales the strike distance by `(50% / vol) × √(week / time left)` and reads a one-week, 50%-vol table (1.25× margin). So cover gets cheaper as its expiry nears, costs more for longer terms (four weeks double the effective volatility move), and costs more when BTC is volatile. At 10% below spot, one full week costs 16.0% of cover and four weeks cost 57.4%.
3. **Volatility from Mezo itself.** `poke()` samples Mezo's PriceFeed (every 15 minutes or more; buys and triggers sample too) into an EWMA of annualised variance with a one-day time constant, clamped to 35–200%. Gaps over a day restart sampling instead of producing one huge sample. No external vol oracle and no owner input.
4. **Touch.** Anyone calls `trigger(expiry, strike)` when `PriceFeed.fetchPrice() <= strike` before expiry, and earns 0.1% of the touched cover (max 10 MUSD). Mezo's oracle keeps no price history (`getRoundData` is not supported), so a touch only counts if someone is watching. The reward pays outside bots to watch, and the app shows a **Trigger it and earn** button to any visitor while a strike is touched. One-touch, not European: cover pays when the danger happens, not on Thursday.
5. **Pay.** Anyone calls `claim(id, hints)`; holders have a **Pay out now** button, so nobody has to wait for a keeper. Mezo verifies the borrower's signature and burns the vault's MUSD against the trove debt. If the repay would fail, the MUSD goes to the borrower's wallet instead. That happens when the signature was voided, the trove is closed, or the repay would break the 1,800 MUSD minimum debt.
6. **Re-arm.** Mezo uses one nonce per borrower for every signed action, so a later signed Mezo action voids the payout signature. The app dry-runs the repay as the vault on every refresh. If it would fail, a **Re-sign** button calls `setAutoRepay` with a fresh signature. For the same reason, of several armed policies only the first to pay can repay the loan; My cover says so when a borrower holds more than one, and the rest pay the wallet unless re-signed.
7. **Expire.** Untriggered cover lapses at its Thursday 00:00 UTC expiry. Collateral is locked per expiry (`lockedCover[expiry]`); expired weeks drop out of `totalLocked()` by themselves, so nothing needs rolling over. The NAV mark of open cover decays with time left, so premiums flow to underwriters smoothly instead of jumping at expiry.

## Why it is deep on Mezo

- **MUSD is the whole balance sheet:** premiums, collateral and payouts are all MUSD; the vault share is a composable ERC-4626 token.
- **Mezo's `PriceFeed`** is the only price source, for triggers, NAV and volatility alike. It is the same feed that decides liquidations, so cover triggers on exactly the price that matters.
- **`BorrowerOperationsSignatures.repayMUSDWithSignature`**: the payout lands inside the trove without the borrower being online. The vault never owns the trove. The app and keeper dry-run that exact call (an `eth_call` from the vault) to show whether a payout will reach the trove.
- **Recovery-mode-aware liquidation line:** the app reads `TroveManager.getEntireDebtAndColl`, `getTCR` and `checkRecoveryMode`. It shows the price at which a trove really becomes liquidatable: 110%, or 150% once the whole system would fall into recovery mode first (recovery starts at `price × 150% / TCR`). It then suggests a strike just above that line and a cover that lifts the trove back to 150%.
- **Sorted-trove hints:** the keeper computes `HintHelpers.getApproxHint` + `SortedTroves.findInsertPosition` for the post-repay NICR.
- **Multicall3 on Mezo testnet** (`0xcA11…CA11`, deployed at block 3,669,328): the app and the keeper batch their reads into one `eth_call`. An app refresh went from 21 RPC requests to 5 while also reading every sold strike of every live week.
- **Mezo Pools** (router `0x9a1f…CD7E9`, BTC/MUSD pool `0xd16A…6AA9`): a borrower holding BTC but too little MUSD for the premium can swap just enough BTC on Mezo's own DEX from the Protect tab. The app finds the amount with the router's `getAmountsOut` (fee and price impact included, stepping up 1% until the quote covers the premium), approves Mezo's BTC ERC-20 precompile and calls `swapExactTokensForTokens` with a 1% floor. It also shows the pool's BTC rate against Mezo's oracle; on testnet the pool trades about 5% under it, and a gap like that is itself a stress signal.
- **Epochs match Mezo Earn:** weekly expiry at Thursday 00:00 UTC.
- **Smart accounts:** Mezo checks repay signatures with plain ECDSA, so smart accounts (such as Mezo Passport's Bitcoin-wallet accounts) can't pre-sign. The app detects contract accounts and pays them to the wallet instead.

## Security model

- **Fully collateralised, capped exposure.** `buy` reverts unless the cover fits `coverCapacity()`: open cover across all four expiries may lock at most `maxUtilization` (50%) of the vault's MUSD net of what is owed. That is the most underwriters can lose. Untriggered cover is locked until expiry; triggered cover is reserved until paid (`totalOwed`).
- **NAV marks open risk.** `totalAssets = balance − owed − Σ cover × premiumRate(price, strike, time left)` (a touched strike counts in full, including its trigger reward), using the same function that priced the premium. Buying cover is NAV-neutral, so new depositors cannot scoop premiums and leavers cannot dodge a coming payout. The mark decays with time, so there is no expiry jump to snipe. A stale oracle marks open cover at 100% and blocks deposits.
- **Withdrawals** are capped at free liquidity. Pausing stops new sales only; it never blocks exits, triggers or claims.
- **Buyer protection:** `maxPremium` slippage bound; the app simulates every transaction before sending it and explains reverts in plain words.
- **No meta-transactions, no owner powers over funds.** The owner can only update the premium table (bounded, non-increasing), set the utilization cap (≤100%) and pause sales. Volatility comes from Mezo's feed, not from the owner. The Oct 5 veBTC exploit came through a trusted-forwarder path; Payung has none.
- **Claim griefing (found and fixed in review):** `claim` is permissionless and the caller picks the gas and the sorted-trove hints. A fork test against the live MUSD contracts showed that a claim sent with ~840k gas made a nested call inside Mezo run out of gas, the repay reverted, and the vault paid the *wallet* instead of the loan, which could leave a borrower to be liquidated. No gas check can tell that apart from a real refusal (bad hints make SortedTroves walk the whole list), so now only the holder can take a failed repay to the wallet at once; anyone else gets `Payung: repay failed` for 6 hours (`FALLBACK_DELAY`), while keepers retry with good hints. `test_fork_claimGasCannotDivertRepayToWallet` sweeps gas limits from 820k to 3M as an outsider and checks no successful claim pays the wallet.
- **Relayer scope:** `api/relay.js` checks with OrangeKit's factory that the target is the Safe `predictAddresses(owner)` derives for the Bitcoin owner the caller names, that the call is `execTransaction` with operation 0, no value and no gas refund, that the inner call is one Payung needs (any Payung v2/v1 function; MUSD `approve` to Payung only; BTC `approve` to the Mezo Pools router only; a router `swapExactTokensForTokens` that pays the Safe itself; no transfers), that the Safe exists, and that a simulation from the relayer succeeds. Sends go through a nonce manager (one relayer key serves everyone) with one retry on a nonce clash, and after a Safe deployment the relayer answers only once the RPC serves the Safe's code, because OrangeKit reads it right away. A Bitcoin wallet that switches accounts (Unisat, OKX announce it) is disconnected with a notice, since it would sign for another Mezo account. The Safe verifies the Bitcoin signature itself, so the relayer can't make an account do anything its owner didn't sign; the most an abuser can do is spend the relayer's (testnet) gas on Payung calls that owners signed. CORS allows the Vercel app, the GitHub Pages mirror and localhost.
- **Strike-slot squatting:** each expiry sells at most 32 strikes, so opening a strike nobody has bought yet takes at least 100 MUSD of cover (`MIN_NEW_STRIKE_COVER`); filling every slot to lock others out is no longer nearly free.
- **Vol manipulation:** samples come only from Mezo's oracle, at ≥15 minute spacing, and each one weighs about 1% at that spacing. A floor of 35% keeps calm markets from pricing cover near zero.
- **Inflation attack:** ERC-4626 virtual shares with a 6-decimal offset.
- **The app can't be framed or hijacked by injected scripts.** The page asks for wallet signatures, so a hidden-frame clickjack would be a real attack. The Vercel deployment sends `Content-Security-Policy` with `frame-ancestors 'none'` (plus `X-Frame-Options: DENY`), `script-src 'self'` and a `connect-src` limited to Mezo's RPC, explorer and Passport relay (test.mezo.org) and ntfy.sh. It also sends `nosniff`, a strict referrer policy and a permissions policy. `vercel.json` is the one source: `vite preview` serves the same headers, so every e2e run (including the fork test, whose local RPC is added only there) runs under the production CSP. The GitHub Pages mirror can't set headers; Vercel is the canonical URL.

The tests found and fixed real bugs:
- Refreshes stacked up on a slow RPC, and one transaction could leave the UI stuck on "Withdrawing…".
- A payout that fell back to the wallet was reported as "into your loan". The message now comes from the `PolicyPaid` event.
- The raw viem error showed for a wallet out of gas money.
- Secondary text failed the WCAG contrast minimum.
- Transactions sometimes "never confirmed" even though they were mined in seconds. The cause: Mezo's public RPC is load-balanced, and some of its nodes answer `eth_getTransactionReceipt` with `null` for a mined transaction (we caught one doing it for minutes while the explorer and `eth_getTransactionByHash` showed it mined). A kept-alive connection can stay pinned to such a node. The shared `waitForReceipt` polls the RPC and, after a few seconds, also reads status and logs from the explorer's API. The app shows "Sent, waiting for Mezo to confirm" with the explorer link as soon as a hash exists, and reads the payout's destination from that receipt.
- The keeper decided whether a volatility sample was due from values read at the start of its pass, but `trigger()` takes a sample too, so a pass that triggered a strike then sent a `poke()` that reverted "too soon" and marked the run failed. It now reads the last sample time and the chain's clock right before deciding.
- The keeper treated any failed repay dry-run as "the repay would fail", including an RPC timeout, and then paid the wallet instead of the loan. A claim is final, so a slow RPC could cost a borrower their auto-repay. Now only a real revert counts, and a timeout makes the keeper retry on the next tick. The fork test caught this: a repay dry-run on a fork takes more than viem's 10 s default.
- The 1% premium slippage bound was too tight: a 0.05% BTC tick during the approval moved a near strike's premium 1.2%, so buys reverted "premium moved" on the live testnet. The bound is now 3%; the contract still charges the actual premium.
- Quote requests raced on a slow RPC: an older answer (another strike or period) could land after the newer one, so the button offered a premium for different inputs and the buy reverted "premium moved". A quote now only counts for the exact inputs on screen, re-quotes when a volatility sample lands, and is re-read right before signing.
- "Premiums on open cover" and the activity feed read the explorer's newest 50 logs. The keeper's 15-minute volatility samples pushed cover sales off that page within hours, so the figure would have quietly shrunk. Both now come from the explorer's Etherscan-style `getLogs`, one event at a time, over the vault's whole life.
- The explorer's log API, past 1,000 results, returns the newest 1,000 (and ignores `page`/`offset`), not the oldest. A reader assuming otherwise would silently drop a vault's oldest deposits once an event passed 1,000, skewing earnings. `explorerLogs` now pages backwards by block, and a live test checks it against Mezo's own `eth_getLogs`.
- A strike BTC had already touched in a cover period could still be picked: the contract's `quote` doesn't check it, so the app showed a premium and the buy reverted "strike already touched" after the approval. The app now reads every sold strike and blocks touched ones (and a 33rd new strike in a full period). The fork e2e test lets BTC recover above a touched strike and checks the warning.
- Several reverts showed raw, including the races with the keeper: triggering a strike someone else just triggered, or paying cover the keeper just paid. Each now has a plain explanation.
- A strike under the loan's liquidation line was accepted silently, though Mezo would refuse the repay there. The app now warns, and the e2e test checks the warning.

## Run it

```bash
npm install
npm run dev                                   # app on http://localhost:5173
KEEPER_KEY=0x... npm run keeper               # long-running keeper: 5 s polling
node keeper/keeper.mjs --once                 # one pass (what the GitHub Actions cron runs)
node keeper/check.mjs                         # read-only: is each auto-repay still armed?
cd contracts && forge script script/Deploy.s.sol --rpc-url mezo_testnet --broadcast --private-key $PRIVATE_KEY
```

The app reads the vault address from `shared/mezo.js` (`?vault=0x…` overrides it in `npm run dev` only, because in a shared build a link could aim MUSD approvals at any contract).

## App features

- **Wallets and network.** EIP-6963 wallet discovery (a picker when several are installed), plus **Bitcoin wallets through Mezo Passport** (Unisat, OKX, Xverse): the picker lists any that are installed, and the app then acts as the wallet's Mezo smart account: its loan, cover and alerts, a reconnect on reload without a prompt, and Disconnect. Transactions are signed by the Bitcoin wallet and checked on-chain by the account (an OrangeKit Safe). Mezo's own Passport relay refuses other sites (CORS behind Cloudflare, and OrangeKit can't be pointed elsewhere), so Payung builds the smart account with its own transaction sender and **runs its own relayer** (`api/relay.js`): the first transaction deploys the Safe from OrangeKit's factory, and every later one is an `execTransaction` the relayer submits and pays for. Writes go through the account's own `sendTransaction`: viem's `writeContract` with an address account would ask Mezo's node to sign. It uses OrangeKit (the layer under `@mezo-org/passport`) directly, without RainbowKit, and loads it only when a Bitcoin wallet is picked, so the main bundle doesn't grow. A Passport account is treated as a smart account from the start (before its first transaction deploys it), so its cover pays the wallet. Its phone alerts are signed in Bitcoin (BIP-137, tag `btcsig-`): readers recover the Bitcoin owner from the signature and ask OrangeKit's factory which Safe it owns, so My cover shows *Signed by you* only for the account's own key (`shared/btc.js`; a live test publishes an owner's alert, another key's alert and a tampered one to ntfy.sh and checks each). Also `accountsChanged`/`chainChanged` handling, and a wrong-network banner with one-click switch or add.
- **Loan-aware defaults.** A recovery-mode-aware liquidation line and a suggested strike and cover from your trove.
- **Cover period.** One to four weeks, each with its own live quote.
- **Live pricing.** A premium quote that falls as the expiry nears. It shows the on-chain volatility and the 3% slippage bound (a strike 8% below spot reprices about 25× as fast as BTC, so a 0.05% BTC tick moves the premium over 1%).
- **Payout check.** A warning when the strike sits so low that, even after the payout, the loan would be under 110%. Mezo would refuse that repay.
- **Underwriter view.** Vault value, utilization, premiums this week and free liquidity. A share-price line since launch is read from the vault at 25 past blocks (Mezo's RPC serves archive `eth_call`), with the change since launch and, once a week has passed, the simple (not compounded) annual pace. Compounding the first day's premiums over a year printed +191,698,721,092%. Vault activity, including owner actions (cap, premium table, pause), is decoded from every event since deployment through the Mezo explorer's (Blockscout) `getLogs`.
- **Your earnings.** Position value + withdrawn − deposited, from your own `Deposit`/`Withdraw` events.
- **Price freshness and loan health.** "BTC now" shows when Mezo's oracle precompile (`PriceFeed.oracle()`, `latestRoundData().updatedAt`) last updated. Your collateral ratio and how far BTC sits above your liquidation line.
- **Paused sales.** If the owner pauses new cover, a banner says so and buying is disabled; everything else keeps working.
- **Calendar reminder.** Each active policy has an **Add to calendar** link: an `.ics` event at expiry with an alarm a day before and a renewal link (RFC 5545 escaping and line folding).
- **Renew in one click.** **Renew for the next week** (or **Buy again** for expired cover) opens Protect with the same strike and cover for the week after; a renewal link (`?strike=&cover=&expiry=`) does the same. If BTC moved and the old strike is outside today's 5–30% band, the nearest one is picked and the app says so.
- **How likely is a touch?** Two independent reads, side by side with the premium. First, a model: the reflection-principle touch chance at the vault's on-chain volatility for the time left. Second, a backtest: 30 days of BTC read from Mezo's own oracle (~360 archive `eth_call`s to the oracle precompile, JSON-RPC batched into a handful of requests), counting the stretches in which BTC fell that far within the same time. The 30-day path is drawn against your strike and liquidation line. When 30 days hold only one or two stretches of that length, it says so.
- **Wallet niceties.** **Add pyMUSD / MUSD to my wallet** (EIP-747 `wallet_watchAsset`, with the vault's real 24 decimals) so the share token shows next to the user's BTC. A wallet that already allowed the site reconnects on reload from `eth_accounts`, without a prompt; **Disconnect** forgets it.
- **Read-only view of any wallet.** `?address=0x…` opens the app as that wallet with nothing connected: its loan, liquidation price, cover, payout routes, calendar reminders and the alerts on its phone topic, with every button that would sign disabled. An advisor or teammate can check a borrower's protection, and judges can look at real policies without a wallet, e.g. [the e2e wallet](https://payung-ecru.vercel.app/?address=0xe42F6c4b72577CE2A64D583CF2f33a2D729c9C81).
- **If BTC falls (stress test).** For each strike the vault sold, how much it pays in total if BTC falls that far before the cover ends, and the share of the vault, from the vault's own strike book. Under it, the **expected payout**: each open strike's cover times its chance of a touch before that cover ends (the same on-chain-volatility model the Protect tab shows), next to the premiums collected for that cover, so underwriters see whether the book is priced above its expected loss.
- **My cover.** Per-policy auto-repay health from a dry-run of Mezo's repay, with a one-click re-sign and **Pay out now** for touched cover. Active cover whose strike the loan's liquidation line has since passed (the borrower borrowed more or took BTC out after buying) is flagged: the loan would be liquidated before it pays.
- **Anyone can keep.** Touched strikes show a **Trigger it and earn** button with the reward. The underwriter view shows when the price was last sampled, so a stalled keeper is visible.
- **Redemption queue.** Mezo redeems MUSD for BTC starting at the loan with the lowest collateral ratio. The Protect tab shows where your loan stands in that queue and how much debt is redeemed before it (e.g. "44 of 277, 116,707 MUSD first"), and weighs it against **every redemption Mezo has processed** (the TroveManager's `Redemption` events, from the explorer): 23 on testnet since Aug 2025, the largest 2,695 MUSD, and how many of them would have reached your loan in today's queue. It warns when one redemption the size of the largest would reach you, and the keeper sends covered borrowers in that spot a signed phone alert (at most once a day; an e2e test on an anvil fork moves a real covered loan to the front of the queue and checks the real keeper alerts it). The queue position is one `eth_call` that runs the creation code of [`RedemptionQueue.sol`](contracts/src/RedemptionQueue.sol), a lens that is never deployed and walks every sorted trove inside the call. A unit test checks it against a manual walk of Mezo testnet's `SortedTroves`, and CI checks the app's copy of the bytecode matches the source.
- **Payout preview.** The Protect tab shows your collateral ratio at the strike, before and after the payout.
- **Phone alerts with the page closed** ([ntfy.sh](https://ntfy.sh), open source, no account or key). Each borrower has a topic derived from their address, shown in My cover with a link and a **Send a test alert** button. The keeper publishes at ntfy's urgent priority (it rings through Do Not Disturb on Android) when BTC comes within 3% of a live strike and when it pays a policy (saying whether the MUSD went into the loan or the wallet), and at high priority when a covered loan comes within reach of a redemption (see Redemption queue). A failed alert never blocks a trigger or a payout. ntfy.sh topics are public: anyone can read one (nothing the chain doesn't show) and anyone can post to one, so alerts are **signed**. Each is an EIP-191 signature over topic, tag, title and text. My cover lists recent alerts with who signed them: *Signed by the Payung keeper*, *Signed by you* (the test alert, signed by your wallet) or *Not from Payung, ignore it*. Alerts carry no links and never ask you to sign. The keeper's "already sent" check, which stops a restarted cron run repeating an alert, counts only alerts it signed itself, so posting a tag first can't suppress one. A live test publishes a keeper alert, an unsigned copy of its tag, a replayed signature on altered text and an alert from another key, and checks that only the first is attributed to the keeper.
- **Browser alerts.** In an open tab, a notification when BTC is within 3% of your strike and when cover pays out. They go through a small service worker (`public/sw.js`, registered only once alerts are on) because Android Chrome refuses `new Notification`, and a refused alert can never take the page down. Alerts already shown are remembered per account across reloads, and payouts from before this browser was watching are not announced as news. A test replays Android's "Illegal constructor" in all three browsers.

## Known limits

- **The fallback window starts at the trigger.** `FALLBACK_DELAY` counts from `triggeredAt`, not from the first failed repay. If every keeper is down for 6 hours after a touch, a stranger could then starve a still-valid repay into the wallet payout. The holder can always claim (and repay) themselves, and the GitHub keeper plus anyone running `npm run keeper` retry every few seconds; anchoring the window to the first failure needs another vault version.

- **Redemption risk is a warning, not cover.** We designed a cover that pays when a loan nears the front of Mezo's redemption queue and dropped it: the queue position can be moved cheaply by the buyer. Borrowing more moves your own loan forward (blockable by recording the ratio at purchase), but opening throwaway loans ahead of your own, buying cover and closing them drops the debt ahead below any threshold for the price of gas, and on-chain nothing tells "other borrowers left" from "my own loans left". Paying only when a loan is actually closed by redemption is harder to fake but tiny (anyone at the front could redeem against themselves whenever the payout beats the redemption fee). So Payung shows the queue position against Mezo's real redemption history and phones covered borrowers who come within reach, and leaves the cover for a trigger that holds up.

- **Payung's relayer for Bitcoin wallets** (`api/relay.js`, a Vercel Function with its own key) pays the gas for Passport accounts. It only relays OrangeKit Safe deployments and Bitcoin-signed `execTransaction`s that call Payung, MUSD, BTC or the Mezo Pools router (see Security model), so it can be drained only of gas, and only for calls users really signed. If it runs out of testnet BTC, Bitcoin wallets can't send until someone tops it up (`GET /api/relay` shows its balance).

- **Touch detection** depends on someone calling `trigger` while BTC is under the strike. Mezo's oracle exposes only the latest round, and the Pyth contract on Mezo testnet holds a months-old BTC price whose Hermes history API now needs a key, so a past dip can't be proven on-chain. The GitHub Actions keeper polls every 10 s inside each run, but GitHub starts scheduled runs on a best-effort basis (we saw none fire in the first hours), so it is a backstop, not a guarantee. `npm run keeper` polls every 5 s on any always-on machine. The trigger reward exists so outside bots watch too.
- **Premium table** shape is fixed (one week, 50% vol); vol and time enter through the distance rescaling. The owner can refresh the table.
- **Mezo testnet quirks** we hit:
  - `eth_estimateGas` undershoots calls that touch the oracle precompile, so we send a fixed 3M gas limit (gas costs ~150 wei).
  - `PriceFeed.fetchPrice` occasionally reverts on an oracle timestamp underflow; the keeper retries.
  - `eth_getLogs` is limited to 10,000 blocks, so the app reads history from the explorer API.

## Integrations looked at and not used (yet), with the reason

- **Pyth on Mezo testnet** (`0x2880…7B43`, BTC/USD feed `0xe62d…5b43`) could let `trigger` prove a past touch with any signed price from inside the cover week, instead of needing someone to look while BTC is down. Its stored price is months old, and Hermes, the only source of signed updates, now answers 401 without an API key. It is the next change once there is a key.
- **MUSD Stability Pool** (`0x1CCA…680e`) as yield for idle vault MUSD: `withdrawFromSP` reverts while any trove is under 110%, which is exactly when cover pays out, so parking backing there would block payouts.
- **veBTC gauges / Mezo Earn incentives:** gauges are for pool LP tokens and need Mezo governance to add, and MUSD is not a whitelisted incentive token on testnet.
- **ERC-1271 repay signatures for smart accounts:** `BorrowerOperationsSignatures` checks `ECDSA.recover == borrower`, so a Safe or Passport account can't pre-sign; their cover pays the wallet.
- **Gelato / Chainlink Automation:** no Mezo support found; the GitHub Actions keeper (plus anyone running `npm run keeper`) stays.
